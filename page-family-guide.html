Start with one product page, such as Landscaper Insurance. From that single page you can plan audience pages, a Town/City + Coverage version of each, one review, and a list of blog posts. The examples below use a made-up agency, Harbor Insurance, so the names stay clean. The titles are samples of the kind of idea an ask returns. An ask writes its own titles.

Out of scope

  • Office location pages, and the older location-product pairs, stay as they are. A Town/City + Coverage ask does not create an office page.
  • This guide does not put audience pages, Town/City pages, Town/City + Coverage pages, or review pages into the menu.
  • Nothing here publishes a site by itself.

One page, followed all the way through

This is the smallest loop. One audience page, then one Town/City + Coverage page, with the review and one blog beside them.

  1. Hub, already live. Landscaper Insurance. The buyer stored on later pages is Landscaper. That word is the title with Insurance taken off the end. If a title does not end in Insurance, the buyer is the whole title.
  2. Ask for audience ideas. Audience tab. Choose Landscaper Insurance. Ask. Twelve ideas come back when twelve are available. One of them is Workers' Comp for Landscapers. Checking it does not create a page. Starring it does not create a page. The star is for this client only.
  3. Push that one idea. The Audience tab gains an unpublished page. The article is queued. The page remembers two things: buyer Landscaper, and parent Landscaper Insurance. It shows up as a card on Landscaper Insurance only after a person publishes it, and only after Landscaper Insurance itself is previewed or published again.
  4. Ask for Wayland. Town/City + Coverage tab. Town/City Wayland, state Massachusetts. Because the audience page exists, the list can offer Workers' Comp for Landscapers in Wayland, Massachusetts. If you have no audience pages yet, the list is empty. A Town/City + Coverage ask will not invent a Town/City + Coverage page from the hub alone.
  5. Push the Wayland idea. Two things are created if they are new. The Town/City + Coverage page, linked to the audience page it came from. And the Town/City page, Insurance in Wayland, Massachusetts, with one article queued. The Town/City + Coverage card sits on the Town/City page. It does not sit on Landscaper Insurance.
  6. Ask for the review. Coverage Review tab. Pick Landscaper Insurance. You get one suggestion. Push it. The row title is Landscaper Insurance review. The article is queued. The page has a quote form. It is not a card, and it is not in the menu.
  7. Ask for a blog. Blogs tab. Seed landscaper insurance. Push Does a landscaper need workers' comp in Massachusetts? That creates a blog draft and queues the article. It does not create an audience page, and it does not fill a card grid.
  8. A person publishes each draft. The hub is published again so the audience card is on it. The Town/City page is published after the Town/City + Coverage page, so the Town/City + Coverage card is on it.

Asking again

  • Ask the same product page for audience ideas again. New ideas are added. Ideas already in the list are not repeated. Pages you already pushed stay.
  • Ask for the same place again. You get audience pages that still have no page for that place, up to 12.
  • Ask the same product page for a review again, after you have pushed it. You are told the guide is already on the schedule. A second page is not created.

Where you click

Content Schedule tabs, in order: All, Product Pages, Blogs, Audience, Town/City + Coverage, Coverage Review, Publishing History. The schedule opens on Product Pages. All is a look-only list: name, type, publish date, and authors. Do the asking, pushing, and publishing on the matching tab.

This is the part to remember. One product page is a hub. The first ask gives you a dozen audience pages. Each of those can be rewritten for every town or city you care about. One review and a stack of blogs sit beside that. A calm quarter of publishing can be a single topic.

The modest pass

Stay with Landscaper Insurance. Take the first audience ask. Give those pages to three towns or cities. Add the one review. Write the blogs from one keyword. Count:

That is about 72 new pages from one hub: 12 + 36 + 3 + 1 + 20. The hub was already published, so it is not in the 72. The menu still shows the one product page. The other pages are reached from cards, from the Town/City page, from the review’s own address, and from the blog.

Ask for audience ideas a second time and you get up to 12 more. Three towns or cities would then want up to 36 more Town/City + Coverage pages. That second ring is real. It is also why a month should take a slice, not the whole tree.

How a town or city multiplies the list

You have published, or at least pushed, those eight audience pages. You type Wayland, Massachusetts. The ask offers those eight, because none of them has a Wayland page yet. Push six. You now have:

  • Workers' Comp for Landscapers in Wayland, Massachusetts, linked back to Workers' Comp for Landscapers.
  • The same pattern for the other five you pushed.
  • One Town/City, Insurance in Wayland, Massachusetts, with a single article. The six Town/City + Coverage pages card onto it after they are published and the Town/City is published again.

Framingham is the same move. The audience pages already exist, so Framingham can be asked the same week you ask Wayland. Each town or city is a copy of the audience list, not a new topic.

Three towns or cities and all twelve audience pages would be 36 Town/City + Coverage pages plus 3 Town/City pages. That is the dozen-into-dozens step. You do not have to do it in one month.

Blogs are the flexible layer

A blog seed is a keyword, not a parent page. “landscaper insurance” can return ideas like these:

  • Does a landscaper need workers' comp in Massachusetts?
  • What a landscaper truck policy often leaves out
  • How to insure a mower and a trailer
  • What happens when a mower throws a rock
  • Certificates of insurance for a commercial property

About twenty come back. Push a handful each week. A second seed, “landscaper workers comp”, is another twenty. Leave that second list unpushed and next month already has a shelf.

Use a blog when the piece answers a question. Use an audience page when the piece should sit on the hub as a coverage someone can buy. Use a Town/City + Coverage when that same offer should name a town or city. Use the review when you want one checklist for the whole program.

What a quarter looks like

At 25 published pages a month, three months is 75 pages. The modest pass above is about 72 new pages. One topic can fill a calm quarter.

At 40 a month, that same pass is a bit under two months. The third month can start the next hub, such as Plumber Insurance, or take the second audience ask and one more town or city.

How to run the month

Both examples start the same way. Harbor Insurance already has Landscaper Insurance published. No landscaper audience pages exist yet. You want a steady public site, and a shelf of ideas and drafts for the month after.

Three piles, every week:

  • Ideas. Asked, not pushed. Free to keep. They are not pages.
  • Drafts. Pushed. The article is writing or written. The page is not on the site.
  • Live. A person published it.

Push a few more pages than you publish. Read them before they go live. The writer does one page at a time, so a push of eight is an afternoon in line, and a push of forty is a long line. Spread the pushes across the weeks. The cap on one push is 100.

A 25-page month

One town or city. Most of the first audience list. A short blog list. Friday is the publish day in this exercise. Move the day if you like. Keep the shape: ask early in the week, push a batch you can read, publish a set number.

Week 1 · Open the buyer

  • Audience tab. Pick Landscaper Insurance. Ask. Twelve sample ideas come back. The list is in the One topic tab.
  • Push the first eight: General Liability, Workers' Comp, Truck, Equipment, Umbrella, Tools and Mowers, Business Owners Policy, Inland Marine. All “for Landscapers”.
  • Leave four ideas unpushed: Seasonal Crews, New Landscaping Businesses, Snow Removal, Lawn Care Only.
  • Blogs tab. Seed landscaper insurance. About twenty ideas. Push six. Two samples: “Does a landscaper need workers' comp in Massachusetts?” and “What a landscaper truck policy often leaves out.”
  • Put this Friday on four audience rows and two blog rows.
  • Publish 6: 4 audience pages and 2 blogs. Eight pages are still in the queue from this week’s push.

Week 2 · Finish the hub, start Wayland

  • Publish 6: the other 4 audience pages and 2 blogs. The month is at 12. All eight audience pages are live.
  • Publish Landscaper Insurance again so those eight cards show on the hub.
  • Town/City + Coverage tab. Wayland, Massachusetts. The ask lists the eight audience pages. Push six of them. Leave Umbrella and Inland Marine without a Wayland page.
  • That push creates Insurance in Wayland, Massachusetts and queues its one article.
  • Push three more blogs. Nine blogs are now pushed.

Week 3 · Ship Wayland

  • Coverage Review. Pick Landscaper Insurance. Push the one suggestion, Landscaper Insurance review.
  • Push the last two Wayland pages, Umbrella and Inland Marine, so eight Town/City + Coverage pages are in drafts or live. You will publish six and hold two.
  • Push three more blogs. Twelve blogs are now pushed.
  • Publish 7: 4 Wayland Town/City + Coverage pages, the Wayland Town/City page, and 2 blogs. The month is at 19.

Week 4 · Close the month, keep a shelf

  • No new ask. Read what is left.
  • Publish 6: 2 Wayland Town/City + Coverage pages, the review, and 3 blogs. The month is at 25.
  • Publish the Wayland Town/City page again so the six live Town/City + Coverage cards are on it.
  • Still in drafts, on purpose: 2 Wayland Town/City + Coverage pages and 3 blogs.
  • Still ideas, on purpose: 4 audience ideas and 8 blog ideas.

A 40-page month

Same hub. Same first audience ask. You keep two more audience pages, you open a second town or city, and you ask for a second blog list that you do not push. The week still publishes about ten pages, so reading stays possible.

Week 1 · Take almost the whole audience list

  • Ask on Landscaper Insurance. Push ten of the twelve. Leave Snow Removal and Lawn Care Only as ideas.
  • Blog seed landscaper insurance. Push six.
  • Publish 10: 6 audience pages and 4 blogs.

Week 2 · Finish the audience pages, open Wayland

  • Ask Wayland. Ten suggestions, one per audience page you pushed. Push all ten. The Wayland Town/City page is created and its article is queued.
  • Push four more blogs. Ten blogs are now pushed.
  • Publish 10: the other 4 audience pages, 4 Wayland Town/City + Coverage pages, and 2 blogs. All ten audience pages are live. Publish the hub again so the ten cards show. The month is at 20. Six Wayland drafts are still writing.

Week 3 · Finish Wayland, add the review

  • Push the review for Landscaper Insurance.
  • Push three more blogs. Thirteen blogs are now pushed.
  • Publish 10: the other 6 Wayland Town/City + Coverage pages, the Wayland Town/City page, the review, and 2 blogs. The month is at 30.
  • Publish the Wayland Town/City page once the six new Town/City + Coverage pages are live, so all ten Town/City + Coverage cards are on it. If the Town/City page went live earlier in the week, publish it again after the last Town/City + Coverage pages.

If the 25-page month already happened

Do not rebuild it. Eight audience pages, a Wayland Town/City page, and a review may already be live, with five drafts and twelve ideas on the shelf. A following busy month then spends its 40 on the shelf, on Framingham, and on the next hub. The counts above are for a month that starts from the hub alone, so the two examples can be compared on the same footing.

A pace you can keep

  • Publish about the same number every week. Six in the calm month. Ten in the busy month. A week that publishes twenty will not get read.
  • Audience pages before Town/City + Coverage pages. The Town/City + Coverage list is made of audience pages you already have.
  • One new town or city per calm month. Two in a busy month. A third town or city is thirty-six Town/City + Coverage pages once you have twelve audience pages. Save it.
  • One review per hub, whenever the audience pages for that hub are underway. It does not multiply.
  • Blogs fill the gaps. If a Town/City + Coverage batch runs long, publish blogs that week and move two Town/City + Coverage pages to next week. The month’s shape survives.
  • End the month with drafts and ideas still unused. That is the next month’s head start.

The honest limit

The pages multiply faster than a person can read them. Twelve audience pages across three towns or cities is already thirty-six Town/City + Coverage pages, before blogs. The machine will queue them. It will not decide which ones deserve to be on the site. The monthly plans exist so the queue stays smaller than the tree.

Open on purpose

  • The twelve audience titles and the blog titles in this guide are samples. An ask writes the real ones. Keep the ones that match the agency. Delete the rest. Deleting an idea does not delete a page you already pushed.
  • A hand-added page is allowed when the list missed one. On the Audience tab it still belongs to the product page you picked. On the Town/City + Coverage tab it still stores the audience page it came from and the Town/City page it sits on.

Why can one topic become dozens of pages?

Because the pages are combinations, not new subjects. One hub has many audience slices. Each slice can be written for many towns or cities. The review happens once. Blogs are a keyword list beside that. Twelve audience pages and three towns or cities are already thirty-six Town/City + Coverage pages.

What is the buyer?

The product page title with the word Insurance taken off the end. Landscaper Insurance stores the buyer Landscaper. Every audience page from that ask is a Landscaper page, and the cards sit on Landscaper Insurance.

Where does a visitor open a Town/City + Coverage page?

From the Town/City, Insurance in Wayland, Massachusetts. The Town/City + Coverage is not in the menu, the gallery, Related Coverages, or coverage search. The Town/City stays out of those places too.

Where does a visitor open an audience page?

From a card on the product page. The card shows the title, one sentence when you have stored one, and a Learn more link. The list has no cap. Unpublished audience pages are left off it.

Why is there only one review?

The review is the single walkthrough for that coverage, with a quote form. A second one for the same product page is refused. It is not another audience page, and it is not shown as a card.

Does Push to Planner publish?

It creates the unpublished page and queues the article. A person publishes later. Checking an idea, or starring it, does not create the page.

What does the schedule date do?

It marks the day that row is due, so the month stays spread out. Push is what lines up the writing. The date does not put the page on the site.

What if I ask again?

An audience ask or a Town/City + Coverage ask adds new ideas and keeps the pages you already pushed. A review ask, once that guide is on the schedule, tells you it is already there.

What if the suggestion list missed a page?

Add it by hand. An audience page still belongs to the product page you chose. A Town/City + Coverage page still needs its audience page and its Town/City page. A duplicate title on the same parent is refused.

Do blogs feed the Town/City + Coverage pages?

No. A blog is its own article from a keyword. Planning audience and Town/City + Coverage pages does not create blog posts. Pushing a blog does not create an audience page.

What should I do after the first topic is well covered?

Start the next product page, or take a second audience ask and one new town or city. Taking every remaining combination in one month is how the queue gets ahead of the reading.
