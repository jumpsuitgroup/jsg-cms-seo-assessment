The inversion the audit wants vs what we built

Checklist governing principle: structural quality automatic, content uniqueness mandatory.

Structural quality Content uniqueness
Checklist Schema, canonicals, breadcrumbs, rendering — machine-enforced Publish blocked on stubs, geo-tokens, cross-tenant dupes
JSG CMS today Mostly automatic (static export, JSON-LD, NAP, SEO templates) Explicitly not compared. REQ-CGE-006.3: uniqueness via context + block-plan variance; “The system shall not perform cross-client embedding comparison.” Starter pages can publish. targetCity/targetState exist — the doorway-page primitive.

That is the single most important finding. Closing the uniqueness gap is not a new feature on a blank slate — it is a product-decision reversal.

3. Four recorded conflicts (resolve before writing WOs)

Do not extract work orders for these until a human accepts the reversal. Silent divergence from decisions.md / FRDs is forbidden.

C1 — Cross-tenant duplicate detection is forbidden today

  • Checklist P0: shingle/embed body on publish; hard-block >80%, warn >60%; per-tenant duplication score.
  • Recorded: REQ-CGE-006.3 — “The system shall not perform cross-client embedding comparison or similarity checks against other clients' pages.”
  • Why it was rejected: cost/complexity; uniqueness via per-tenant context.
  • Why the audit overrides it: one shared starter library across N tenants is scaled-content abuse. Prompt context does not catch “foundational liability coverage” copied with a city swap.
  • Recommendation: amend CGE. Keep context-driven generation; add a publish-time comparator as a second line of defense. Do not replace generation uniqueness with detection — stack them.

C2 — Flat URLs vs hierarchical URL enforcement

  • Checklist P0: /{category}/{product}/, /locations/{city-state}/{product}/, /learning-center/{slug}/. Editors choose a parent, not a raw path.
  • Recorded: Round 4.10 — product, blog, ads, landing pages are flat /${slug}/ (pathForPage, content.flatSlugs.test.ts). Collision guard exists; hierarchy does not.
  • Recommendation: do not flip URL shape until a redirect engine exists. Hierarchical URLs are Stage C, gated on Stage A redirects.

C3 — Hedge vs quote

  • Checklist P1: hedge-word linter (may help, typically, in most cases) because hedged sentences are unquotable by AI.
  • Recorded: compliance stage flags definitive language and tells reviewers “Insurance copy should hedge.” (ComplianceFlagService, complianceLabels.ts).
  • This is a real YMYL tension, not a bug. Insurance sites must not promise coverage. Answer engines must still extract a sentence.
  • Recommendation: split the rules. Quotable definitions (what GL is / is not) stay unhedged and live in the Answer Summary. Advice/eligibility stays hedged. Linters apply per field, not per page.

C4 — NAP validator vs per-location phones

  • Checklist P0: per-location phone is first-class; NAP is a single source of truth per location.
  • Code: validateNap() warns if a page body mentions any phone whose last 10 digits differ from the agency phone.
  • If we add location phones without changing this, every location page will fail NAP.
  • Recommendation: evolve the validator to an allowlist of entity phones (agency + each location), still blocking free-text numbers that match neither.

6. What this means for sequencing

Do not start with location×product generation or hierarchical URLs. Those amplify whatever uniqueness and redirect story we have. Today uniqueness is unenforced and redirects don't exist — so building the matrix first would manufacture doorway pages with no 301 safety net.

Sequence by what the audit said is highest leverage intersected with what we can land on current primitives without reversing architecture.

9. What I would not do

  • Do not generate 50 city pages from targetCity before uniqueness gates.
  • Do not change URL hierarchy before redirects.
  • Do not treat Authors as “E-E-A-T done.”
  • Do not treat CGE context-uniqueness as a substitute for duplicate detection.
  • Do not put hedge-word lint on the whole page and fight compliance.
  • Do not SSR the public sites. Static export already satisfies “SSR or statically generate.” The remaining job is CI + no JS-only meaning.
  • Do not wait for a full SEO-health dashboard before the publish scorecard. The scorecard is the gate; the dashboard is the report.

10. Bottom line

The CMS is already a better technical SEO platform than the WordPress builds the audit tore apart. Static HTML, real CTAs, NAP-from-intake, JSON-LD, sitemap, llms.txt, authors with real URLs, staging noindex, citations, and a human publish gate are real.

It is not yet the platform the audit describes as the competitive moat:

  1. Uniqueness is optional (and comparison is banned).
  2. Reviewer + review date — the cheap E-E-A-T differentiator — do not exist.
  3. Answer Summary is a prompt wish, not a field.
  4. Location is a list on one page, not an entity graph.
  5. Enforcement that exists (verify:seo, verify:nojs) is not in CI. Product/FAQ checks in verify-seo.ts still look for /products/ and /lp/ — they no longer match flat URLs.
  6. Licenses and founding-year equity sit in intake and never reach the footer.
  7. publishedAt is Payload updatedAt, not a real publish date, and is not in JSON-LD — a save bumps “published.”
  8. Public pages have no <main> / skip link; tracking “head” slots actually inject at the top of <body>.

If the team can only do three things after Stage 0: put the existing verifiers in CI and stop shipping hardcoded stats (A), ship YMYL reviewer + answer summary + stub/dupe gates (B), then build location×product behind those gates (C). That is the audit's leverage order, mapped onto this codebase.
